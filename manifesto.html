The manifesto

At the beginning,
no one knows who
they will become.

Not even the one who asks.

Every human being is born with a potential that cannot be imposed, manufactured or defined by anyone else. It can only be discovered.

Curiosity is where learning begins — not obedience, not talent, not instruction. A question.

Emotion is the door through which knowledge enters, and art and science are two ways of asking the same thing.

One story can change the way a child looks at their own future, because sometimes a life turns in an instant — and that instant can begin with a story.

Marie Curie, in her story, does not know she is Marie Curie. Mozart does not know he is Mozart.

They are children, as uncertain as any child alive, about who they might turn out to be. No narrator will tell them. No mentor will know for them. No music will announce what is coming.

Because the moment anyone inside a story knows a child’s future before the child does, we have already taught the wrong lesson: that potential is granted, rather than found.

We do not dream of children who admire great people.We dream of children who dare to ask the same question about themselves.

We are not here to be admired.We are here for the instant a child looks up from a question and thinks: I could do that.

The one law above every other decision

The protagonist and the one watching discover the world at the same time.

What will never be permitted here

To ridicule curiosity. To narrow a child’s dreams. To impose one way of thinking. To manipulate an audience through its feelings. To underestimate the intelligence of children. To trade purpose for commercial success.

We do not make content.
We make founding moments.

Where this comes from

The opening of the creative constitution of the house: thirteen chapters that decide what may belong to this universe and what may not. It is closed until the first work is finished.
